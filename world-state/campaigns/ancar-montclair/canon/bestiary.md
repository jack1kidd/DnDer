# Bestiary — What Lives On The Larenne Moor

Glenumbra is not a wilderness. It is grazed, cut for peat, walked by drovers, and
crossed twice a week by the Daggerfall coach. Nothing on the Larenne moor is
waiting to be slain by heroes. Most of what can kill a man here is either an
animal defending itself, weather, or the ground.

Keep this small. A doorsman at the Hollow Horn will hear about these creatures far
more often than he will meet one — they belong in the taproom, in complaints about
lost ewes and a carter's broken leg, in what mothers tell children about the causeway
after dark. When one does step into a scene, it should be sudden, brief, and
disproportionately serious, then over. This is not a rotation of set-piece monsters.

## Beasts, plainly

**The hedge pack.** Moor wolves, lean and grey-brown as the gorse, denning somewhere
north of the peat-cuts. In summer they take lambs and are cursed at. In a hard
winter they come down to the hedgerows along the coaching road and follow lone
travellers for miles without ever quite closing — which is worse than being attacked,
and which most Larenne folk will tell you they have experienced personally, whether or
not they have. A lone walker at dusk in Sun's Dusk or Evening Star has a real chance
of dying out there. A party with a fire and a spear does not.

**Gorse-boar.** The animal that actually kills Larenne men. Old boars go blind-mean in
the whin thickets south of town; they weigh what a man weighs and travel faster,
and a tusk opens a thigh in a way the chapterhouse's restoration can only half mend.
The barony licenses two boar-hunts a year, both of which end at the Horn, both of
which have at some point ended with someone carried in.

**Mire adder.** Short, dun, patterned like wet bracken, and lethargic enough that
almost every bite in the parish has been the bite of someone who stepped on it during
hay-cutting. Seldom kills a grown adult; reliably ruins a fortnight, and reliably ruins
a child. Peat-cutters carry a length of cord to bind an arm above the bite and
consider this sufficient medicine.

**Gallow-rooks.** The moor's carrion crows, glossy and unhurried, notable only because
Larenne reads them: rooks circling a particular stretch of the moor road means someone
should go and look, and by long custom somebody does. They are the town's alarm bell
and its gossip in equal measure.

## Ground and weather, which count as beasts here

**The peat-sinks and the haar.** Cut peat leaves black water under a skin of moss, and
the sea-fog comes off the Iliac in an hour and takes the world down to six feet. The
causeway stones are laid for exactly this reason and stepping off them is how most
people who die on the moor die. No violence, no monster — just cold water, a wrong
turn, and a body found by the rooks in the spring.

## The two things nobody quite claims are animals

Kept ambiguous. Do not confirm, do not stat, do not build an encounter around them.
Their function is to explain why Larenne does not cross the moor after dark, and to
sit at the same volume as the PC's own quiet wrongnesses.

**Fen-candles.** Pale lights over the wet ground beyond the peat-cuts, seen most
often in the still hours before rain. Any Breton hedge-wife will tell you frankly
that it is marsh-gas and nothing else, and she will say so while making the sign
of the Eight, because the other thing everyone knows is that the lights sometimes
travel with you at your own walking pace, and sometimes they are on the causeway
side, and sometimes a man who followed one is found in the sink come spring. Perhaps
twice a generation. Enough.

**The tallow hound.** A long low dog the colour of rendered fat, seen on the moor road
between the last hedgerow and the old manor's gate. Nobody in living memory has been
bitten by it. The belief is only that it walks beside you a while, keeps your pace,
does not breathe audibly, and turns off at the gate — and that whoever it walks with
buries someone before the year is out. Larenne treats this as superstition in daylight
and as settled fact at night, and both positions are held by the same people.

**A note on the old manor.** In late summer, moths gather on its shuttered windows in
numbers that people remark on and then stop remarking on. That is all. It is a detail
for later, not a creature.
