# Of the Powers, Charters, Houses and Covenants of the Empire in the Year 3E 430

*Being the fourth volume of the* Provincial Concordance *of the Imperial Geographical Society, compiled by Brother Aldos Venim, cloister-scribe, who wishes it noted that he compiled it under protest and without adequate lamp-oil.*

---

## I. The Elder Council, and the Question No One Asks

The Elder Council convenes in the White-Gold Tower as it has for four centuries: forty-odd seats of provincial lords, ministerial appointees, and two guild proxies, arranged in a horseshoe beneath a ceiling of the Nine ascending into gold leaf. The business of the Council is grain tariffs, road-tolls, legion quartering, and the endless litigation of the Breton duchies. The business *beneath* the business is the succession, and it is conducted entirely in the negative — in who is not invited to which supper, in which minister's clerk is suddenly reassigned to Leyawiin, in the exact half-second of silence that follows any sentence beginning "when His Majesty is at last relieved of his burdens."

Uriel Septim VII is seventy-nine and has ruled for fifty-one years. He is not senile; that rumor is put about by men who would find it convenient. He is *elsewhere* — increasingly given to long silences in Council, to dismissing the chamber mid-debate, to dreams he describes to his Blades and to no one else. The Empire has heirs: **Prince Geldall**, the eldest, thirty-four and diligent to the point of dullness; **Prince Enman**, who prefers Bravil and the Nibenese salons; **Prince Ebel**, who is nineteen and prefers horses. Blood is not the problem. *Consent* is the problem. The Council has, in the last two years, discovered that it enjoys the interregnum of an aging Emperor rather more than it will enjoy a young one.

Four currents run in that chamber, and a player who cannot name them will not last a season at court:

**High Chancellor Ocato of Firsthold** holds the Empire together with procedure. He is Altmer, born in Summerset before the Isles closed, and he has served the Septim line for forty years with a legalist's cold devotion. His agenda is continuity — Geldall crowned, the machine unbroken, nothing decided in a hurry. His method is the agenda-order: Ocato wins by scheduling. His enemies do not accuse him of treason. They accuse him, more effectively, of *ears* — of being an Elf in the year the Thalmor opened an embassy, and of having taken tea at it twice.

**Grand Marshal Cassius Vitellan** holds the Legion seat, a Colovian of the Gold Coast garrisons who has spent thirty years being told by clerks what the army may afford. He believes, with the total sincerity of a man who has never once been wrong in his own hearing, that Tamriel is held by soldiers and that the next Emperor must be one. He is not plotting a coup. He is doing something more patient: touring the provincial garrisons, learning which legates answer his letters within the week, and letting the arithmetic sit there.

**Councilor Ariante Mena**, Minister of Provincial Affairs and a Nibenese of the old river families, plays the opposite hand — she would buy the provinces' loyalty by *giving them things*: Morrowind's House law confirmed in perpetuity, Skyrim's jarl-moot recognized, Hammerfell's Crowns granted their own seat. Her critics call it selling the Empire in pieces. She calls it, in her one recorded moment of candor, "paying for the peace we have been stealing."

**Countess Narina Carvain of Bruma** sits for the northern march and trades in relics and leverage in roughly equal measure. She wants the Pale Pass properly garrisoned before Skyrim's jarls remember it exists, and she will vote with whichever bloc funds it. She is the Council's honest broker chiefly because both blocs need her and neither can afford her.

And then there is the thing the Blades know. **Grandmaster Jauffre**, who keeps a monastery's habit at Weynon Priory outside Chorrol and a spymaster's ledger under it, has been quietly verifying a matter for three years. The Emperor has spoken — once, in Council, and then never again — of a son the Council has never seen. Ocato struck it from the record as a misheard word. Jauffre did not.

## II. The Four Charters, or, Where Power Actually Sits

The Empire's genius was never the Legion. It was the charter: give a trade its own law, its own dues, and its own internal politics, and it will police itself more thoroughly than any garrison. Two of the four great guilds are chartered. The other two are the reason the first two matter.

### The Mages Guild
Chartered under the Emperor's own seal, seated at the **Arcane University** on the Imperial Isle, with halls in Chorrol, Bruma, Anvil, Leyawiin and every provincial capital that will have one. It teaches the six schools — Alteration, Conjuration, Destruction, Illusion, Mysticism, Restoration — to any applicant who can pay or place, regardless of birth, which is precisely why Skyrim's jarls loathe it and why every third ambitious farm-child in Cyrodiil walks to the Imperial City.

The Guild is currently having its own succession, and everyone in Council has noticed the rhyme. **Arch-Mage Aurelia Sallustis** is ninety-one, brilliant, and has refused for eleven years to rule on the necromancy question — the Guild's oldest fault line, since the Guild's own founding charter names conjuration of the dead a school of study and not a crime. The Council of Mages splits. **Hannibal Traven**, of the Skingrad hall, would ban the practice outright, expel its practitioners, and burn the relevant shelves; he is the reformer, and by most reckonings the next Arch-Mage. **Councilor-Mage Caranya**, Altmer and formidable, argues that a ban would hand the Empire's best necromantic scholarship to the **Order of the Black Worm** and its King of Worms — an argument that is entirely correct and, whisper the University's more suspicious apprentices, entirely self-serving. **Raminus Polus** runs the University's day-to-day recommendations and is the man an outsider actually meets: patient, overworked, and generous with the sort of errand that turns into a career.

*Friction:* the Guild pays a charter-tithe to the Elder Council and holds a Council proxy seat through **Councilor Irlav Jarol**. Traven's proposed ban would require the Council's ratification. Vitellan has already offered to deliver the Legion's votes for it, in exchange for battlemages seconded to the provincial garrisons. Ocato has not yet let the item onto an agenda.

### The Fighters Guild
Chartered as a mercenary company: monster culls, caravan escort, guard-work, debt-recovery that stops short of the debtor. Its heart is **Chorrol**, where **Master Vilena Donton** presides — a competent administrator and a mother, in that order, ever since her elder son died on a Guild contract and her younger, **Viranus**, insisted on taking the badge anyway. She has spent two years quietly assigning him work that cannot possibly kill him, and the Guild has noticed. Her Second, the Dunmer **Modryn Oreyn**, holds the Guild's actual standards together with contempt and paperwork, and would have the Master's chair in a heartbeat if the Guild's charter did not make it hereditary in practice.

*Friction:* the **Blackwood Company**, out of Leyawiin, under the Khajiit **Ri'Zakar**. They undercut Guild rates by a third, take contracts the Guild's charter forbids, and complete them. Their men come back from the Blackwood marshes glassy-eyed, unwounded, and very calm, and nobody has yet explained what they drink. Leyawiin's Count has licensed them. Vilena Donton has petitioned the Elder Council twice. Councilor Mena, who wants Leyawiin's tariff vote, has twice failed to table it.

### The Thieves Guild
Unchartered, deniable, and therefore the only one of the four the Empire cannot audit. Its code is three lines and older than the Septims: steal from anyone; never kill in the work; pay the Guild its cut. Its doyens hold court in the Imperial City Waterfront (**Armand Christophe**, who interviews applicants at a dockside grave and expects them to have already stolen something), in Anvil's harbor cellars, and in Bravil. Above them all is **the Gray Fox**, who has led the Guild for three hundred years, which is either proof of a Daedric bargain or proof that the Guild has had the sense to keep re-issuing the same mask.

*Friction:* **Captain Hieronymus Lex** of the Imperial Watch has made the Guild a personal crusade, and has begun — this is the dangerous part — actually being good at it. The Waterfront has been swept twice this season. And in Morrowind the Guild has no code-protection at all: the **Camonna Tong** kills its fences on sight, and the two organizations' war has cost more lives in Vvardenfell this decade than the Empire's tax collectors have.

### The Dark Brotherhood
Not a guild. A covenant, sworn to **Sithis** — whom the Imperial Cult files among the Daedra for want of a better shelf, and whom the Brotherhood's own scriptures insist is the thing that was there before there were shelves. A contract is opened by the **Black Sacrament**, a rite of rotted flesh and nightshade performed over an effigy for as long as it takes to be answered. The **Night Mother** hears; the **Listener** — an unremarkable Bosmer in Bravil named **Ungolim**, though no living Speaker will confirm it in words — receives; the **Black Hand** disposes. **Lucien Lachance**, Speaker for the Cheydinhal Sanctuary, does the recruiting, and his method is simple: he watches for someone who kills without a contract and offers them the chance to never do it for free again.

*Friction:* the Brotherhood and the Thieves Guild maintain a professional non-aggression that both sides describe as respect and both sides mean as *avoidance*. In 3E 428 a Waterfront fence was found with a nightshade sprig in his mouth, and the Gray Fox's response was to close two safehouses and say nothing. The Brotherhood has also — and this is the item that ought to worry the Council — accepted three contracts in eighteen months on minor Elder Council functionaries, all clerks, all in the Ministry of the Purse. Somebody is thinning a ledger.

## III. The Quiet Congregation

There is a cult in the Imperial City that nobody important has heard of, and this document will be the first to make the mistake of writing it down.

The **Mythic Dawn** teaches that the Empire's peace is a cage the Divines built to keep men small, that the Aedra gave up their power to make the world and have nothing left to lend, and that a Prince — Mehrunes Dagon, though the cult prefers "our Lord" until the fourth interview — will one day send a champion through a door to unmake it. It has been recruiting since about 3E 415, and it recruits *upward*: not from the poor, but from bored second sons, disillusioned Cult acolytes, and mid-ranking Imperial clerks who have read enough Council minutes to conclude that the Empire is a filing error.

Entry is a reading list. Four volumes of the *Mythic Dawn Commentaries*, ascribed to one **Mankar Camoran** — a name that would mean a great deal more if anyone at court connected it to the Camoran Usurper's war of 3E 397, and no one has. The first three circulate freely among collectors; **Phintias**, who keeps the First Edition bookshop in the Imperial City Market District, will sell you all three and complain about the binding. The fourth does not circulate. The fourth is a *reply* — you leave word that you are looking for it, and something finds you.

The cell's contact in the capital is a Ministry of the Purse tax-clerk named **Vaesa Malleius**, forty-one, unmarried, who audits the Waterfront's slip-fees on Fredas and takes tea afterward at the same terrace on the Talos Plaza. The cult calls her rank *Sponsor*. She has never done anything illegal and never will. She simply notices who is angry and introduces them to a book.

Nothing has happened yet. That is the point. In 3E 430 the Mythic Dawn is a curiosity for antiquarians, a footnote in three Watch reports, and an unpursued line in a Blades ledger — and the only person in the Empire treating it as a serious matter is a Bosmer bookseller's client who paid four hundred septims for a pamphlet.

## IV. The Impeccably Correct Embassy

The Summerset Isles closed themselves in the last century, and the government that closed them — the **Thalmor** — maintains in the Imperial City a small legation of eleven staff on the Talos Plaza, whose conduct has been, in the twelve years of its existence, entirely and pointedly beyond reproach. It pays its dues early. It attends every state function and dances badly on purpose. It has never once been caught doing anything.

**First Emissary Ilianwe Tarnaril** presents the Dominion's compliments and asks questions that sound like small talk for eleven years running: which legions rotate through Anvil, which noble houses have Altmeri debt, which of the Emperor's sons has an interest in ships. Her attaché, **Estarion Vaele**, catalogues — literally, in a ledger the Blades have twice failed to steal — every mention of Talos-as-god made in an official Imperial proceeding. Neither has ever said what the ledger is *for*. The embassy's stated position on the Nine Divines is that the Dominion respects the religious customs of its neighbors. It is the word *neighbors* that Jauffre keeps underlining.

*Friction:* Ocato is Altmer, and every meeting he takes at that embassy costs him three votes among the Talos-hardliners. The Thalmor know this. Chroniclers dispute whether they seek him out for information or simply to bleed him. And there is one item nobody will put in writing: the embassy's junior scribe has twice been seen at Phintias's counter, asking after a book that is not for sale.

## V. The Provinces, Which Do Not Consider Themselves Provincial

**Morrowind** governs itself by treaty through its Great Houses, and the treaty is the whole quarrel. **House Hlaalu** grew rich on the Empire's roads and the Empire's law; its councilor **Crassius Curio** — poet, lecher, and by a wide margin the shrewdest man in Balmora — holds Morrowind's Council proxy and sells his vote for tariff exemptions with a frankness the Nibenese find refreshing and the Colovians find obscene. **House Redoran**, under **Archmaster Athyn Sarethi**, keeps the old honor and the ancestor-tombs and considers Hlaalu a house of foreign clerks wearing Dunmer faces; Redoran has begun, this year, refusing Imperial garrison rights at three of its border forts and calling it a scheduling matter. **House Telvanni** does not attend. Its wizard-lords rule from towers by sorcerous strength alone, and its one reform-minded voice, **Master Aryon**, argues that a House which ignores the Empire will be legislated out of existence by clerks it never bothered to meet — an argument his peers find charmingly modern and beside the point. Beneath all three runs the **Camonna Tong**, native-supremacist, skooma-funded, and married to Hlaalu money through **Orvas Dren**, whose brother happens to be the Duke.

**Skyrim** answers to a High King in Solitude, **Rangvar Cold-Crown**, whose authority over the jarls is roughly the authority of a man holding a rope tied to nine dogs. The jarldoms pay Imperial tax and honor Imperial law where the road runs, which in Skyrim is not far. **Jarl Sigdrifa Bear-Widow of Falkreath** holds the Pale Pass road to Bruma and has doubled its toll twice, which Countess Carvain considers a border incident and Sigdrifa considers commerce. **Jarl Kjeld the Unmourned of Windhelm** keeps the Grey Quarter's Dunmer refugees behind a curfew and speaks openly of the Hearth-Oath — the old claim that Morrowind owes Skyrim blood-price for raids two eras settled. The Mages Guild's Skyrim halls operate on sufferance and a great deal of insurance.

**Hammerfell** is three factions pretending to be two. The **Crowns** — the old Na-Totambu bloodlines, strongest in Hegathe and Stros M'Kai, led in argument if not in title by **Rahd al-Marek** — hold that the Redguards conquered Hammerfell before the Empire existed and owe it nothing but courtesy. The **Forebears**, seated in Rihad and Sentinel's ports, hold that the Empire's peace built the docks that feed them; **Admiral Idris al-Rihad** speaks for them at sea and is, not coincidentally, the reason Anvil's pirates retired. Between them, **King Lhotun of Sentinel** has invented a third position — honor the Old Ways *and* the treaty — and the **Lhotunics** are consequently hated by both sides with a purity neither can manage toward the other. Councilor Mena's proposal to grant the Crowns their own Council seat would, everyone agrees, either settle Hammerfell or set it alight.

## VI. The Nine, and Their Weathervanes

The **Imperial Cult** keeps a chapel to the Nine in every city worth the name, and its priests are pastoral counselors, Restoration healers, and — this is the part the chapels do not advertise — the finest intelligence network in Tamriel, since everyone confesses to somebody. **Arch-Prelate Sercia Vellani**, who keeps the Temple of the One in the Imperial City, has spent two years compiling a return she has not yet submitted to the Council: attendance is down in the capital, markedly, among exactly the sort of educated young Nibenese who used to fill her fourth pew. She has asked her deacons where they went. The deacons say: reading.
