# Twilight of the Third Era — A Field Guide to the Rules

*Being a Digest of the Realm's Governing Principles, Compiled for the Instruction of Adventurers, by Brother Aldos Venim of the Imperial Geographical Society. Errors of fact are the Society's; errors of judgment are the reader's own.*

---

This campaign runs a familiar chassis — d20 rolls against a Difficulty Class, classes and levels, hit dice and armor class — dressed, top to bottom, in Tamriel's own clothes. The chassis is scaffolding. What follows is the building.

## I. The Well, Not the Slot

Every caster, regardless of class, draws from a single pool of **magicka**. There is no Vancian memorization, no slot expended and forgotten by dawn — only a Well, and how carefully you draw from it. Casting costs magicka by the spell's tier (a cantrip-tier working is free; a 9th-tier miracle costs 13). Run dry and you may still cast, at a price paid in blood and exhaustion — **Overdraw the Well** costs 2 HP per point of magicka you lacked, and a rising Fatigue that courts disaster.

## II. The Two Lenders

All magic in Tamriel is borrowed. The question is only from whom, and what the interest is.

**The Aedra** diminished themselves at the world's making, and what they lend — Restoration, most Alteration, a priest's blessing — is honest, finite, and never quite free. Spend too freely and you owe a temple an *Obligation*: a tithe, a vigil, a rite performed properly, within seven days.

**The Daedra** lend generously and collect eventually, on their own schedule, in the fiction rather than on the character sheet. A Prince-Sworn's bargain, a Conjurer's summons, a Daedric artifact picked up without a second thought — all of it opens a **Ledger**. When the Ledger fills, the Prince collects. Never a stat penalty. Always a scene.

And a slain Daedra is never truly dead — only sent home, remembering your face.

## III. The Six Schools

Alteration, Conjuration, Destruction, Illusion, Mysticism, Restoration. Every spell in the game belongs to exactly one, and your access is gated by who taught you — a Guild hall, a hedge-witch, a Telvanni tower — not by a generic class list. Rank in a school (Novice through Master) gates the tier you may cast, and rank rises only through training, loot, or a teacher owed a favor.

## IV. Guild Rank Spends Like Coin

Membership in the Mages, Fighters, or Thieves Guild, or the Dark Brotherhood, is a tracked resource with real leverage: Favors to call in, Debt that accrues when you do, and a code that costs you dearly to break. It advances on its own track, parallel to character level — a modest fighter may already be a guild Champion, and both facts matter equally at the table.

## V. Race Is the Room's Reaction

Your race is not a stat line; it is what every stranger in the scene decides about you before you speak. Nords distrust open magic. Dunmer call you outlander. Khajiit are searched at the gate. This is played, not rolled past — and it comes with real cultural obligations (the Green Pact, Malacath's Code, House law, blood-feud, manred) that cost you standing to break.

## VI. Thirteen Signs, One Sky

Everyone is born under a sign, and everyone asks. It is Tamriel's small talk and grants one real, permanent power — from the Warrior's opening-round edge to the Atronach's dangerous, magicka-hungry gift.

## VII. Luck Is Spent, Not Rolled

The seventh attribute. Burn it to turn a failure into a success, or force a reroll against you. It does not return on a night's rest — only by leveling up, a shrine's boon, or a Prince's favor (which, naturally, opens a Ledger).

## VIII. The Cull-Book

Before the party commits to a fight, the world tells them what it's worth: the Fighters Guild grades every threat from **Vermin** (coppers a pelt) through **Standing Bounty**, **Charter**, and **Sealed**, up to **No Contract Issued** — the Guild's own way of saying *walk away*. This is your telegraph. Heed it.

## IX. The Empire's Peace, and the Thinning

Two clocks tick beneath this campaign whether or not the party notices. **The Empire's Peace** measures the realm's stability — succession anxiety, provincial unrest, a cult's quiet growth — and edges toward a year the history books already remember. **The Thinning** measures something older and stranger: the resistance between Mundus and Oblivion, wearing down. Neither stops for the party. Both can be slowed, at a cost.

## X. No Crime Without a Witness

The Watch enforces the Empire's Peace with an ordinary ledger, not omniscience. Bounties accrue only for what's seen — which makes stealth, alibis, and a guild's fence genuinely worth the trouble.
